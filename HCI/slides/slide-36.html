Which Interaction Types Are Best?

The lecture matches different interaction types with different activities:

  • instructing → repetitive tasks;
  • conversation → various accessibility and specialist situations;
  • manipulation → doing and spatial tasks;
  • exploration → learning, socialising and games;
  • hybrid conceptual models → multiple interaction approaches.

Lecture segment

There is no universally best interaction type.

The appropriate interaction depends on the task, user, and context.

Consider deleting a photograph.

We could support:

  • Instructing: press Delete.
  • Manipulating: drag the photograph into Trash.
  • Conversing: “Delete this photo.”
  • Responding: system asks whether old duplicates should be removed.

All could be technically valid.

The HCI question is:

Which is most appropriate for this user and this situation?

Hybrid interfaces

Modern systems commonly combine interaction types.

Google Maps, for example, may support:

  • direct manipulation of the map;
  • text search;
  • voice instruction;
  • system-generated navigation alerts.

This creates flexibility but may also increase learning complexity.
