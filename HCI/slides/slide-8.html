Three Core UCD Principles

Lecture segment

These three principles provide an excellent checklist for student projects.

1. Understand users early

We investigate characteristics such as:

  • behaviour,
  • cognition,
  • physical capabilities,
  • attitudes,
  • tasks.

For example, if we are designing for restaurant kitchen workers, we might discover:

  • they work quickly;
  • their hands may be wet;
  • environmental noise is high;
  • they rarely have time to read lengthy text.

These findings directly influence the interaction design ideas.

2. Measure behaviour

Don't only ask:

“Do you like this prototype?”

Instead observe users attempting tasks.

Measure things such as:

  • completion;
  • mistakes;
  • hesitation;
  • time;
  • requests for help.

A participant saying “Yes, this is easy” while taking four minutes to find the checkout button tells us something important.

3. Iterate

Testing is not the final step before product delivery.

The intended process is:

Design → Testing → Issue detection → Redesign → Retesting

In industrial practice, this process may be repeated dozens or hundreds of times.
