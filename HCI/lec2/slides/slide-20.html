Metaphor Examples

The lecture identifies:

  • desktop;
  • shopping cart and checkout;
  • tabbed interfaces.

Lecture segment

These metaphors are so common today that many users no longer recognise them as metaphors.

Desktop

The computer screen becomes a workspace.

We have files, folders, and a trash/recycle bin.

Shopping cart

Online products behave metaphorically like objects placed into a trolley.

You can:

  • add;
  • remove;
  • inspect;
  • proceed to checkout.

Tabs

Tabs originally resemble physical dividers used to organise information.

Professional question

Whenever you introduce a metaphor, ask:

“Does my target user already understand this metaphor?”

A metaphor only reduces learning when users recognise the thing being referenced.
