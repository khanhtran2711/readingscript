Contexts for Interaction Design

Interaction design is really about designing the relationship between people, activities, and technology.

This means that our job is not:

“Design a screen.”

Our actual job might be:

“Help nurses record medication safely.”

The solution may involve a screen, scanner, wearable device, notification system, or several technologies working together.

Notice the objectives listed on the slide:

  • make work more effective;
  • make work more efficient;
  • make activities safer;
  • improve learning;
  • support entertainment;
  • improve communication;
  • encourage creativity.

These are human outcomes, rather than interface features.

Professional application

When writing project objectives, avoid:

“Develop a mobile application with five screens.”

Instead consider:

“Reduce the time warehouse staff need to locate goods while minimizing errors during inventory data entry.”

The second statement tells us what successful interaction design should achieve.
