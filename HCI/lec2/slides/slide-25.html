Composite Metaphors and Mental Models

The lecture notes that modern interfaces often combine ideas that do not correspond perfectly to physical objects, such as windows and scrollbars, and that people can develop multiple mental models.

Lecture segment

Interfaces do not need to represent one metaphor perfectly.

Think about a browser:

  • windows;
  • tabs;
  • bookmarks;
  • address bar;
  • back button;
  • download folder.

These metaphors come from different physical experiences, yet users successfully combine them into one mental model.

Design implication

Designers have flexibility.

You do not need to say:

“Because I chose a book metaphor, everything must behave exactly like a book.”

Instead, use concepts that help users perform tasks effectively.

Consistency matters, but conceptual purity is not always necessary.
