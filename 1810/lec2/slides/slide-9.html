Slide 9

Pageviews and useful outcomes

Teaching cue: 36 to 40. Use the blog-post chart. Ask students to identify the leaders in pageviews, subscription count, and displayed rate. Allow 45 seconds before explaining the differences.

A pageview records a view of a page. Repeated views can count again. In GA4, the Views metric can include website pages and app screens, so check the streams included in the report. A high view count shows a lot of recorded viewing activity. It is not a count of distinct people. [9]

In the screenshot, the positive-person article leads with 9,486 pageviews. However, the business-questions article has the most subscriptions, with 4,858. The social-media article has the highest displayed rate, at 66.9 percent. Each ranking answers a different question.

Notice something else. The displayed percentages match subscriptions divided by pageviews. For the positive-person article, 2,127 divided by 9,486 gives about 22.4 percent. That denominator is pageviews. We should describe the displayed calculation accurately rather than automatically call it the percentage of readers who subscribed.

To measure the percentage of readers who subscribed, we would need distinct eligible readers and distinct subscribers linked to those readers. Repeated pageviews and repeated subscription events can otherwise change the meaning. We also need to know how the report credited a subscription when a person read several posts.

For UniCart, a headphone guide may attract many visits, while a delivery-information page may help people complete purchases. The delivery page could be valuable even with fewer views. A page’s role in the journey matters when we decide how to improve it.

The supplementary notes suggest monetising popular pages through advertising, affiliate links, or sign-ups. Those are possible choices for a commercial content site. For a university admissions page, we would judge the effect on applicants’ tasks before introducing anything that distracts from essential information.

In a professional review, compare page purpose, entry source, and outcomes before copying the design of the most-viewed page. A popular topic and a successful interface are different possible contributors to the result.

Transition to slide 10: A pageview tells us that viewing activity occurred. Bounce rate asks a more specific question about the session’s measured engagement.
