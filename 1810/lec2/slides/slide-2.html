Slide 2

Website purpose and performance

Teaching cue: 03 to 07. Use the purpose sign on the slide. Give pairs 45 seconds to choose a useful outcome for a university admissions page and for an online shop, then take two brief answers.

The word “purpose” on this slide matters because the same behaviour can mean different things on different websites.

If you visit a university website to check an application deadline, find the answer in twenty seconds, and leave, that may be a successful visit. If you spend twenty minutes searching and still cannot find the deadline, the website has probably made your task harder. For UniCart, we might care about a completed purchase, but we also need to consider whether the customer received the right product and whether the order made a useful contribution to the business.

A dimension describes the data, such as the device or page. A metric gives a numerical measurement, such as sessions or purchase revenue. A KPI is a metric selected to judge progress towards an important objective. That choice gives the metric a particular management role. [1]

For our fictional project, suppose the objective is to improve purchasing on mobile. We might choose the percentage of mobile sessions with a confirmed purchase as the main KPI. We could set a target of increasing it from 1 percent to 1.5 percent over four weeks. Those are proposed classroom targets, not industry benchmarks.

The KPI needs a definition that someone else can reproduce. We must state the time period, the eligible sessions, what counts as a confirmed purchase, and who will review the result. We should also watch a guardrail, such as refunds or contribution per order, so that an apparent improvement does not conceal another problem. Giving everyone a huge discount might raise the purchase rate while making the business lose money.

Now discuss the two websites with your partner. What action would provide useful evidence that the website helped its intended users?

Teaching cue: Suggested responses include a valid application submitted or a useful admissions enquiry for the university, and a confirmed order for the shop. Accept alternatives when students explain the purpose. A brochure download can support the admissions journey, but it does not establish that an application was completed.

The slide uses the word “conversion” for a desired action. We can keep that general business meaning. In current GA4 reports, an important measured action is called a key event. Google Ads also uses conversions for advertising measurement and bidding. These labels matter when we open an actual report. [5]
