Dimensions and scope

Teaching cue: 12 to 16. Point to Browser, Landing Page, and Campaign. Give students 30 seconds to classify the examples in the final question.

A dimension is a descriptive attribute used to organise or group data. Browser can contain values such as Chrome or Safari. Device category can contain mobile or desktop. City can contain Berlin or San Francisco. The dimension is the field, while Berlin is one value in that field. [1]

For UniCart, “purchase rate” tells us about an outcome. “Purchase rate by device category” lets us compare mobile and desktop. If the overall result hides a mobile problem, that comparison may reveal it.

There is a correction to make in the slide’s list. Session duration measured in seconds is a metric. If we create categories such as “under one minute” and “one to five minutes”, those categories can act as a dimension. A number does not automatically make a field a metric either. A product ID such as 1042 is a label. Adding product IDs together would not answer a useful business question. [12]

We also need to ask what level a field describes. This is called its scope. A first-user source describes how an identified user was originally acquired. A session source describes the source assigned to a visit. An event name describes a particular recorded action. Mixing these levels carelessly can produce a convincing table that answers a different question from the one you intended. [6]

Suppose Linh first discovers UniCart through an unpaid Google result and returns on another day through a tagged email link. Her first-user source can remain Google while the later session is associated with email. Both descriptions can be valid. One explains initial acquisition, and the other describes the later visit.

Classify these examples: browser, number of purchases, campaign name, and average basket value. Then tell me which dimension would help us investigate the mobile purchasing objective.

Teaching cue: Browser and campaign name are dimensions. Purchase count and average basket value are metrics. Device category is a useful first breakdown for the mobile objective. Browser, landing page, or session channel may provide a second breakdown.
