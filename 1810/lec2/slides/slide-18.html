With your partner, calculate the latest purchase rate for mobile, desktop, and the whole website. Identify one dimension and one metric. Then write two sentences to the manager: one finding supported by the table, followed by the next check you recommend and why.

Teaching cue: During pair work, ask “What is your denominator?” If a pair finishes early, ask whether averaging the mobile and desktop percentages gives the correct website rate. It does not because the session counts differ.

The latest mobile rate is one percent. Desktop is three percent. Overall, twenty-four purchasing sessions divided by 1,600 sessions gives one and a half percent. The unweighted average of one and three would give two percent, which is incorrect for this combined population.

A defensible finding is: “Purchasing sessions increased from twenty to twenty-four, but the overall purchase rate fell from two percent to one and a half percent. Mobile sessions doubled while mobile purchasing sessions stayed at twelve, so we should investigate mobile traffic and the purchasing journey.”

A useful next check could compare mobile channels and landing pages, validate purchase collection, or inspect checkout errors and performance. The table does not prove that a mobile redesign caused the change, or that a redesign will fix it. It helps us choose where to investigate.

Transition to the closing discussion: The original claim was that more traffic meant a better website. Your analysis can now explain the improvements, the weakness, and the evidence still needed for a decision.

Whole lesson conclusion

Teaching cue: 87 to 90. Give students one minute for the exit response, invite one short answer, then deliver the closing paragraphs. Stay on slide 18.

Before we finish, write a short KPI definition for one website you know. State the objective, the metric, and its denominator if it is a rate. Add one dimension you would use to investigate a change and one limitation the metric cannot resolve by itself.

Teaching cue: A suitable response might select completed mobile purchases divided by mobile sessions, investigate by session channel, and explain that the rate alone cannot identify the cause of a decline. For a university site, accept a valid application or enquiry measure with an appropriate opportunity population.

Today, we used metrics to measure activity and outcomes, and dimensions to examine differences within those measurements. We selected KPIs according to the website’s purpose, then checked how their definitions affected the conclusions.

We also saw how easy it is to misread a report by mixing users with sessions, averaging percentages without their weights, or treating a historical label as a current definition. More pageviews, longer visits, and cheaper clicks can all have several explanations.

When you present an analysis, make the calculation understandable, connect the finding to the objective, and state what further evidence would change the decision. That is the standard to carry into the practical work and into a professional project.
