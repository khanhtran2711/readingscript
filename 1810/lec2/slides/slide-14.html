Slide 14

Pages per session

Teaching cue: 63 to 67. Write the corrected formula. Give students 30 seconds to calculate 1,200 pageviews across 400 sessions, then compare two possible explanations for a high result.

There is an important correction in the slide text. Pages per session equals pageviews divided by sessions. It does not divide page count by session duration, and it does not measure scrolling speed.

If UniCart records 1,200 pageviews across four hundred sessions, the result is three pageviews per session. Time is not part of that calculation. In current GA4, the comparable Views per session metric can include both pages and app screens. For a website question, keep the relevant web data in scope. Repeated views can count. [12]

Look at the historical report. Its overall average is 5.86 pages per session. Branded Paid Search is 8.88, while the separate Paid Search row is 4.61. These are the labels in that report’s configuration. We should not assume that every property uses exactly this channel arrangement.

There is also a small transcription correction in the supplementary explanation. The Referral row in the image shows 5.80 pages per session. The value 5.48 belongs to Direct.

What might 8.88 pages per session mean? Visitors may enjoy browsing several products. They may also keep moving between pages because important information is hard to find. A high value does not choose between those explanations.

Imagine we redesign UniCart so that size information, delivery charges, and product details are available clearly on one page. Customers then need fewer pageviews before buying. Pages per session might fall while purchase completion and satisfaction improve.

The useful project question is whether the path supports the task. Pair this metric with completion, errors, and journey evidence. If the purpose is browsing a catalogue, exploration may be valuable. If the purpose is checking an order status, a short successful path may be preferable.

When combining channels, remember the weighted-average calculation from slide 5. Use total pageviews divided by total sessions. Giving a tiny channel the same weight as a large one can distort the overall result.
