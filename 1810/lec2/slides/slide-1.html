Metrics Dimensions and KPIs

Teaching cue: 00 to 03. Introduce the UniCart example. Show the two periods below and allow 30 seconds for a show of hands or chat response. The table is a classroom illustration, not data from a real retailer.

Today, we are going to practise a task that comes up constantly in analytics: deciding what a number actually tells us about a website.

Imagine you are the analyst for UniCart, an online shop selling headphones and other student essentials. The manager walks into your meeting and says, “Good news. Visits are up by 60 percent. Our website is doing much better.”

Here is the report. In the first seven-day period, we recorded 1,000 sessions and 20 sessions with a confirmed purchase. In the next seven-day period, we recorded 1,600 sessions and 24 sessions with a confirmed purchase. For this exercise, both periods use the same collection rules and each purchasing session contains one confirmed order.

PeriodSessionsSessions with a purchase
Previous seven days1,00020
Latest seven days1,60024

Would you agree that performance has improved? Put your hand up if your answer is yes. Now put your hand up if you need more information.

Both reactions have a reasonable starting point. More sessions arrived, and more purchases happened. However, the proportion of sessions producing a purchase fell. We also know nothing yet about advertising costs or profit.

By the end of today, you should be able to explain that situation clearly without hiding behind a complicated dashboard. We will learn the language of metrics and dimensions, decide which measures deserve to become KPIs, and use them to recommend a next action. Keep UniCart in mind because we will return to this same report at the end.
