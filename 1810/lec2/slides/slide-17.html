Slide 17

Cost per click conversion and customer

Teaching cue: 76 to 81. Explain the mismatch between the formula and chart before using the worked example. Give students one minute to calculate the first two cost measures. Display the final CAC calculation during the debrief.

This slide contains a terminology mismatch that we need to correct. CPC normally means cost per click. The formula shown divides cost by conversions, which calculates cost per conversion. Google Ads commonly calls that average cost per action, or CPA. Always write out the action because a conversion could be a lead, registration, or purchase. [19, 20]

The chart itself is labelled “Average Cost per Click”. It therefore concerns clicks. In the final quarter shown, desktop is roughly seventy-four cents per click, mobile roughly thirty cents, and tablet roughly fifty cents. The labelled overall value is forty-two cents. These are historical figures from 2018 and 2019, not current price benchmarks.

Lower cost per click does not automatically mean lower cost per purchase. A channel may attract inexpensive clicks from people who rarely buy. Another may have more expensive clicks but a higher rate of valuable outcomes. We need the cost and the outcome together before deciding how to allocate money.

Here is a separate fictional UniCart campaign. The campaign spends three million đồng on advertising and produces 1,500 measured ad clicks and thirty attributed confirmed orders. Twenty of those orders represent distinct newly acquired customers. The campaign also has one million đồng of other acquisition costs allocated to those customers. Assume the period, attribution window, and cost allocation match for this exercise.

Average CPCVND 3,000,000 / 1,500 ad clicksVND 2,000 per click

Customer acquisition costVND 4,000,000 / 20 new customersVND 200,000 per new customer

Why does customer acquisition cost use twenty rather than thirty? Because the target unit is a newly acquired customer. Existing customers can make additional purchases, and one customer can place more than one order. Why is the numerator four million? Because this example includes the additional acquisition cost. A report using only advertising spend would need to say so.

The same discipline applies to the ROI measure in your supplementary notes. ROI uses a defined profit and investment-cost basis. Return on ad spend, or ROAS, compares attributed revenue with advertising spend. Revenue divided by ad spend is not a profit calculation. [21, 12]

If a dashboard or an AI-generated summary tells you “mobile is cheaper”, ask for the unit. Cheaper per click, per completed order, or per new customer can lead to different decisions. Also check refunds, attribution, and the completeness of the costs before making a budget recommendation.
