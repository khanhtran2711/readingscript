Slide 13

Loading performance and Core Web Vitals

Teaching cue: 58 to 63. Point out the pageview and sample counts. Use the three current performance measures below. Give students 30 seconds to identify which problem they have experienced: slow main content, delayed response, or a moving layout.

You tap a product link on your phone. The main image takes a long time to appear. Then you tap “Add to basket” and nothing seems to happen. Finally, a banner appears and moves the button just as you try again. These are three different performance problems, even if someone describes all of them as “the site is slow”.

The old Site Speed report shows an average page load time of 3.46 seconds. It also shows 27,560 pageviews and a page-load sample of 2,251. The sample is approximately 8.17 percent of the pageview count. We should not say that every pageview was measured for speed, or assume that the sample is representative merely because it contains many observations.

An average can conceal problems affecting particular devices or connections. We also need a precise definition of what finished loading. Main content appearing, a button responding, and every resource completing are different milestones.

Current Core Web Vitals distinguish loading, responsiveness, and layout stability. Largest Contentful Paint, or LCP, concerns when the largest relevant visible content element is rendered. Interaction to Next Paint, or INP, measures responsiveness to interactions. Cumulative Layout Shift, or CLS, measures unexpected movement of visible content. [14]

MeasurePractical questionGood threshold
LCPDoes the main visible content appear promptly?At most 2.5 seconds
INPDoes the page respond promptly to an interaction?At most 200 milliseconds
CLSDoes visible content stay stable?At most 0.1

These thresholds are evaluated at the seventy-fifth percentile, with mobile and desktop considered separately. A seventy-fifth percentile LCP of 2.5 seconds means about three quarters of measured visits have that LCP or better. It is a different statement from saying that the average is 2.5 seconds. All three measures matter for the overall assessment. [14]

The old GA Site Speed screen is not the current GA4 workflow. For present-day investigation, use field performance data where available, alongside controlled testing. Tools such as PageSpeed Insights can support that work. Sending Web Vitals into GA4 requires a suitable implementation rather than assuming the old report will appear automatically. [14, 15]

For UniCart, compare the same page templates across relevant device groups. Large images, heavy scripts, network conditions, or server delays are possible contributors, but the report does not tell us which one caused a purchase-rate change. A performance investigation and a controlled evaluation can test that explanation.
