Slide 8

Traffic sources channels and acquisition

Teaching cue: 30 to 36. Explain the historical chart, then the current GA4 distinction. Give pairs one minute to choose a comparison for UniCart. If demonstrating GA4, use a prepared Traffic acquisition view for no more than one minute within this block.

When someone arrives at a website, we want to understand how that visit was acquired. A source identifies an origin, such as Google or a named newsletter. A medium describes a method, such as organic search, paid click, or email. A channel groups traffic according to a set of rules, giving categories such as Organic Search, Paid Search, or Email. [6, 7]

For example, google / organic describes an unpaid search source and medium. google / cpc can describe a paid search visit. A properly tagged newsletter link might use student_newsletter / email. Keeping campaign tags consistent helps the reports group visits meaningfully.

The slide mentions AdWords and AdSense together. Google Ads is the current advertiser-facing name for AdWords. AdSense is a service through which publishers can earn money by displaying advertising. They have different roles, so we should not use their names as interchangeable traffic-source categories. [26, 27]

The historical chart shows 24,028 sessions in total. Organic Search accounts for 11,808, or about 49.14 percent. This makes it the largest listed source of session volume in that report. It does not establish that it generated the highest profit. We would need outcomes and relevant costs to investigate that question.

The label Direct also needs care. It may include typed addresses and bookmarks, but it can also appear when usable referral or campaign information is missing. We cannot conclude that every Direct visit came from a loyal customer who remembered the address. [8]

In current GA4, distinguish User acquisition from Traffic acquisition. First-user dimensions answer questions about initial acquisition. Session dimensions answer questions about visits. For our question, “Which channels brought the latest week’s purchasing sessions?”, we should use session-based acquisition dimensions and a clearly defined purchase measure. [6]

Suppose paid social brings 1,000 sessions and email brings 100. Does paid social win? We still need the purchase outcomes, the cost of reaching each audience, and the users’ prior relationship with UniCart. Email may mainly reach existing customers. A higher purchase rate there would not, by itself, prove that moving all advertising money to email will produce the same result at a larger scale.

With your partner, choose two measurements you would place beside sessions to help the manager assess a channel. Then add one limitation to that comparison.

Teaching cue: Accept purchase rate, confirmed purchases, revenue, contribution, and a clearly defined acquisition cost. Useful limitations include different audience intent, attribution rules, small samples, missing campaign tags, and incomplete cost coverage.

When you report a channel finding, record the date range and the scope. “Email had the highest session purchase rate in the latest seven days” is much clearer than “Email is our best channel.”
