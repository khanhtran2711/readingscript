Slide 1

Hypothesis and Significance Testing

Approximately 4 minutes

Good morning everyone.

In our previous statistical discussions, we learned how to describe data.

We looked at ideas such as:

mean,

standard deviation,

normal distributions,

Z-scores,

probability,

and samples.

Today we are going to answer a different question.

Imagine that you work as a data analyst for an online shop.

Last month, the website received about:

10,000 visitors per week.

The marketing team launches a new advertising campaign.

This week, the website receives:

many more visitors.

The marketing manager becomes very excited and tells you:

“Amazing! The campaign worked!”

But can we really make that conclusion from one number?

Maybe.

But perhaps weekly traffic normally fluctuates between 9,500 and 10,500.

So 10,450 visitors might be completely normal.

The question for us as analysts is not simply:

“Did the number change?”

The more useful question is:

“Is the change large enough that we have statistical evidence that something actually changed in the population, rather than seeing ordinary sampling variation?”

That is the purpose of hypothesis testing.

Today we are going to learn how analysts move from:

an observation

to

evidence

and finally to

a statistical decision.

But notice my wording.

I said:

statistical decision.

Not:

“absolute proof.”

That distinction will become very important today.
