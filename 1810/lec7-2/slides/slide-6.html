Slide 6

Bread-Weight Example

Approximately 6 minutes

The slide gives this situation:

The company believes its bread mixer produces bread with an average weight of 10 grams.

Customers question whether the weight is correct.

The slide writes:

[ H_0:\mu=10g ]

[ H_a:\mu\neq10g ]

and describes sample observations being used to investigate the claim.

The important part of this example is not bread.

It is the logic.

Step 1 – Population claim

The claim concerns:

the true mean weight of all bread produced by the process.

We cannot realistically weigh every bread item forever.

So the quantity in our hypothesis is a:

population parameter.

We measure the sample, but we make a claim about the population.

That is why we use:

[ \mu ]

Step 2 – Take a sample

Suppose we measure a sample of 100 bread items.

That sample gives us information about the production process.

Step 3 – Compare evidence with H₀

If the sample average is very close to 10 grams, the data may be consistent with H₀.

If it is sufficiently far from 10 relative to expected sampling variation, we may obtain evidence against H₀.

Lecturer Clarification

The slide gives example values such as:

10.67,

10.88,

10.77,

11.20

and says it is obvious that μ is not 10.

The sample mean is 10.88 g, which is higher than the hypothesised population mean of 10 g. However, this difference alone is not enough to reject Ho. But, It doesn’t mean that their clam is correct. as I mentioned before..
