Slide 14

Why 0.05?

Approximately 5 minutes

The supporting notes explain that:

0.05 is a widely used convention,

0.01 is stricter,

and 0.10 is more lenient.

The appropriate α can vary according to context.

This point is very important.

0.05 is not a universal truth

Nature did not create a rule saying:

0.049 = real.

0.051 = fake.

The threshold is a decision convention.

Why use a stricter threshold?

Imagine testing whether changing the colour of a website button improves clicks.

A false positive may cost some development time.

Now imagine testing whether a new medical procedure has a dangerous effect.

The consequences of making an incorrect conclusion may be much more serious.

Different contexts justify different evidence standards.

Type I versus Type II error

The notes explain the trade-off.

If α is more lenient, such as 0.10, it becomes easier to reject H₀.

That may increase the chance of:

false positives / Type I errors.

If the criterion is very strict, such as 0.01, we demand stronger evidence.

But we may miss real effects more easily in some designs.

That relates to:

Type II errors / false negatives.

You do not need to calculate these errors today.

Just understand that statistical decision-making involves trade-offs.

Data analyst mindset

Do not report only:

“p < 0.05, therefore success.”

Instead think:

What was the hypothesis?

What was the sample?

Was the study properly designed?

How large was the effect?

Is the result practically meaningful?

What uncertainty remains?

Statistical significance is one piece of evidence.

It is not the entire analysis.
