Slide 11

Population, Sample and Statistical Inference

Approximately 6 minutes

The supporting notes illustrate statistical inference as movement from:

Population → Sample → Hypothesis testing → Conclusion about population.

Let's use a university example.

Suppose our population is:

all 20,000 students at a university.

We want to know:

Does using an AI study assistant improve exam performance?

Testing all 20,000 students might be impractical.

So we take a sample.

Perhaps:

200 students.

We collect data.

We analyse that sample.

But our real interest is not only those 200 students.

We want to say something about the larger population.

That process is:

statistical inference.

Web analytics example

Population:

All potential visitors to your website.

Sample:

Users observed during an experiment.

Maybe:

10,000 users see Page A.

10,000 users see Page B.

You observe:

Page A conversion = 5.0%.

Page B conversion = 5.4%.

The question becomes:

Is the difference likely to reflect a genuine population-level difference?

Or could sampling variability produce a 0.4 percentage-point difference even if the pages perform equally?

That is the role of inference.

Important condition

A large sample is not automatically a good sample.

Suppose I want to study all university students but collect data only from:

Computer Science students in one morning class.

I might have 500 responses.

But is the sample representative of the entire university?

Maybe not.

Sample quality matters.

Ask students

Which is necessarily better?

100 randomly selected students,

or

5,000 students selected only from one particular course?

Not automatically the 5,000.

Representativeness matters.

Transition

Once the sample has been collected, significance testing asks how compatible the observed result is with our null hypothesis.
