Slide 12

How Significance Testing Works

Approximately 6 minutes

The notes explain that significance testing asks whether the sample provides sufficient evidence to reject H₀.

We calculate statistical quantities such as:

a test statistic,

and

a p-value.

We then examine whether our observed result would be unusual if H₀ were true.

This phrase is extremely important:

“if H₀ were true.”

Imagine:

H₀ says a coin is fair.

You flip it 10 times.

Result:

6 heads.

Is that strange?

Not really.

You could easily obtain six heads with a fair coin.

Now imagine:

100 flips.

100 heads.

Could a fair coin theoretically produce that?

Yes.

But would it be extremely unusual?

Absolutely.

That is the thinking behind a significance test.

Test statistic

A test statistic tells us how far our observed result is from what H₀ predicts, relative to expected variability.

Different tests produce different statistics.

The slides mention:

Z-statistic,

t-statistic,

Chi-square statistic.

You do not need to master all of those today.

The important idea is:

data → test statistic → p-value → decision.

What does “significant” mean?

In everyday English:

significant often means:

important,

large,

meaningful.

In statistics:

statistically significant

has a more specific meaning.

It means that the observed evidence crossed the threshold defined by our significance-testing procedure.

Something can therefore be:

statistically significant

but

practically tiny.

Example

A website has 10 million users.

A redesigned button improves conversion from:

5.000%

to

5.005%.

With an enormous sample, the difference might potentially be statistically detectable.

But your business still needs to ask:

Is that difference worth redesign costs?

Statistical evidence and business importance are related questions, but not identical.
