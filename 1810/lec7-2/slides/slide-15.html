Integrated Conclusion

Approximately 3 minutes

Let's connect today's lecture into one workflow.

You begin with a research question.

For example:

“Does the advertising campaign increase weekly website visitors?”

Then you formulate:

H₀

the default/no-effect position.

And:

Hₐ

the effect or difference you want to investigate.

If Hₐ says:

less than,

you have a left-tailed question.

Greater than:

right-tailed.

Not equal:

two-tailed.

Then you collect a representative:

sample

because usually we cannot measure the entire population.

You calculate an appropriate:

test statistic

and obtain a:

p-value.

The p-value asks:

If H₀ were true, how compatible is a result this extreme with that assumption?

Then compare p with:

[ \alpha ]

If:

[ p\leq\alpha ]

Reject H₀.

If:

[ p>\alpha ]

Fail to reject H₀.

And remember:

We do not normally say:

“Accept H₀.”

Failure to reject is not proof that H₀ is true.

Final 60-second check

Research question:

“Does the new homepage increase conversion above 5%?”

What is Hₐ?

[ H_a>0.05 ]

Which tail?

Right-tailed.

Suppose:

α = 0.05

and

p = 0.02.

Decision?

Reject H₀.

Does p = 0.02 mean there is a 2% probability H₀ is true?

No.

Does failure to reject H₀ mean we proved H₀?

No.

Excellent.

The key idea from today's class is this:

Data can show a difference, but hypothesis testing helps us judge whether the evidence is strong enough to make an inference beyond the sample.

That is one of the foundations of professional data analysis.
