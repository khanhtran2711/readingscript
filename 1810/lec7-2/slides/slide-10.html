Slide 10

Significance Tests and NHST

Approximately 5 minutes

The PowerPoint introduces significance testing as part of statistical inference from sample data to a population and refers to:

Null Hypothesis Significance Testing — NHST.

Let's break that name down.

Null Hypothesis

We begin with H₀.

Significance

We want to determine whether our observation is sufficiently inconsistent with H₀.

Testing

We use a statistical procedure.

Example

Your website normally receives:

200 visitors per day.

You run an advertising campaign.

A sample of days after the campaign averages:

230 visitors.

Is the increase:

30 visitors

large?

Maybe.

But what if daily visitor numbers normally vary by:

±100?

Then 230 may not be remarkable.

What if daily variation is only:

±2?

Then 230 is extraordinary.

This is why significance is not determined merely by the raw difference.

It depends on how large the observed difference is relative to expected variability.

Key analytical idea

A difference can be:

numerically visible

but

statistically unconvincing.

And with a sufficiently large sample, a very small difference can sometimes become statistically significant.

This is why professional analysts should inspect:

the estimated difference,

variability,

sample size,

and statistical evidence.

Not p-value alone.

Transition

But how can information from a relatively small sample tell us something about a much larger population? That takes us back to statistical inference.
