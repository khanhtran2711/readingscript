Writing Hypotheses in Sentence Form

Approximately 7 minutes

Null Hypothesis (Ho):

    - Represents the default or original assumption. - It suggests that there is **no effect, no difference, or no relationship** between the variables being studied. - It is **assumed to be true** until evidence suggests otherwise. - Common phrases: "no difference," "no association," or "no treatment effect."

**Alternative Hypothesis (Ha):**

    - Represents the **opposite** of the null hypothesis. - It suggests that there **is an effect, a difference, or a relationship** between the variables being studied. - This is the hypothesis that the researcher aims to **test or investigate**. - Common phrases: "difference," "association," or "treatment effect."

**The outcome of Hypothesis Testing:**

    - We either **reject the null hypothesis** (providing evidence for the alternative) or **fail to reject the null hypothesis** (meaning the data didn’t provide enough evidence to support the alternative hypothesis).

**Research Situation:**

    - A **treatment** is known to be **25% effective**. - A **researcher** claims that she has a **new treatment** with **improved efficacy**.

Step 1: Articulate the Research Question in Plain English:

  • Question: Does the new treatment improve effectiveness above 25%?

Step 2: Define the Hypotheses:

  • Null Hypothesis (Ho):
    • "No difference" or "no treatment effect" means that the new treatment is no more effective than the current treatment.
    • In mathematical terms:
    • Ho: The new treatment’s effectiveness is ≤ 25%.
    • Plain English Interpretation: The new treatment does not improve the effectiveness beyond the current 25%.
  • Alternative Hypothesis (Ha):
    • "Difference" or "treatment effect" suggests that the new treatment is more effective.
    • In mathematical terms:
    • Ha: The new treatment’s effectiveness is > 25%.
    • Plain English Interpretation: The new treatment improves effectiveness compared to the 25% of the current treatment.

Step 3: Testing the Hypotheses:

  • The null hypothesis (Ho) will be assumed true unless there is enough statistical evidence to reject it. If the data shows a significant improvement in efficacy, we will reject the null hypothesis and conclude that the new treatment is more effective than 25%.
