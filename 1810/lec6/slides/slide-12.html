Slide 12

Histogram vs Bar Chart

Approximately 7 minutes

This slide is one of the most important in today's lecture.

The notes identify several differences: data type, grouping, bar position, purpose, x-axis meaning and y-axis meaning.

Let's reduce all of that to one fundamental distinction:

Bar chart = categories.

Histogram = distribution of numerical values.

Consider:

Computer Science, Business, Design, Engineering.

Categories.

Use a bar chart.

Now consider:

Exam scores from 0 to 100.

Numerical values.

If we want the distribution, use a histogram.

Difference 2 – Gaps

Bar-chart bars normally have gaps because categories are separate.

Histogram bars normally touch because intervals are continuous.

Difference 3 – Ordering

Suppose your bar-chart categories are:

Facebook.

Instagram.

TikTok.

You could reorder them according to value.

The fundamental meaning still exists.

But imagine histogram bins:

0–10.

10–20.

20–30.

Could we randomly rearrange them?

No.

Numerical order matters.

Difference 4 – Analytical question

Bar chart:

Which category is bigger?

Histogram:

How is this numerical variable distributed?

Transition: Histograms show the distribution of one numerical variable. But sometimes we have many numerical variables and want to see how they relate to one another simultaneously. That brings us to the correlation matrix.
