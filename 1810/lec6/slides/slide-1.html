Slide 1

Data Representation and Visualisation Using Graph and Table

Approximately 3 minutes

Good morning everyone.

Today we are moving from simply having data to actually communicating something meaningful with data.

Imagine I give you an Excel file containing 20,000 rows of online-shopping transactions.

Technically, you have the data.

But do you understand what is happening?

Probably not.

You may want to know:

Which product category generates the highest sales? Are sales increasing or decreasing? What does the typical customer spend? Are there unusual customers? Does advertising spending appear to be related to revenue?

Reading thousands of rows is not an efficient way to answer these questions.

That is why data representation and visualisation are important.

A good visualisation converts numbers into something that our brain can recognise much faster.

Pause and ask the class:

If I show you 5,000 numbers in an Excel worksheet, how long might it take you to understand the pattern?

Probably quite a long time.

But if I turn the same values into a graph, sometimes you can identify the pattern in two or three seconds.

That is the power of visualisation.

And remember something important today:

Creating a graph is easy. Choosing the right graph is the difficult part.

Transition: So before learning individual chart types, we first need to understand how the nature of the data influences our visualisation choice.
