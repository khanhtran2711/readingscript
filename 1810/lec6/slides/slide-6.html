Scatterplot

Approximately 6 minutes

is a visualisation for numerical data that is mainly used to examine relationships between variables,or theri patterns and trends

Look at the example on this slide.

We have:

x = Hours of Sunshine

and

y = Ice Creams Sold

The sample values move from low sunshine and low ice-cream sales toward high sunshine and high ice-cream sales.

Imagine plotting each pair as one point.

What pattern do you expect?

Probably something moving upward from left to right.

That tells us something important.

As sunshine increases, ice-cream sales also appear to increase.

What does one point mean?

This is something beginners sometimes miss.

A point is not just a random dot.

One dot might mean:

5 hours of sunshine and 7 ice creams sold.

Another point might mean:

9 hours of sunshine and 15 ice creams sold.

So every point represents the combination of two measurements.

But here is a very important analytical warning.

A scatterplot can suggest association or correlation

It does not automatically prove causation.

Correlation or association does not automatically mean one variable caused the other. Identifying a correlation is just the starting point; confirming causation requires much deeper analysis.

The scatterplot allows us to ask:

Does there appear to be a relationship?

Is it positive?

Negative?

Strong?

Weak?

Linear?

Or perhaps there is no obvious pattern?
