Approximately 3 minutes

Before we finish, I want to connect everything we covered today.

When you receive a dataset, do not immediately click:

Insert Chart.

First ask:

What am I trying to communicate?

If you need exact numbers, a table may be the best choice.

If you want to compare categories, think about a bar chart.

If you want to understand the distribution of continuous numerical data, consider a histogram.

If you need a compact summary of a distribution and want to identify potential outliers, use a box plot.

If you want to investigate the relationship between two numerical variables, a scatterplot can help.

If you have many numerical variables and want an overview of pairwise linear relationships, a correlation matrix can be useful.

If your main question concerns change over time, a line chart is often appropriate.

And whatever graph you create, remember SALT:

Scale.

Axes.

Labels.

Title.

But there is one final principle that is more important than remembering the names of these graphs:

A visualisation is an analytical argument.

You are choosing what the audience sees.

You are deciding how the numbers are represented.

You are influencing which patterns become obvious.

So good data analysts do not ask only:

“Can I create this chart?”

They ask:

“Is this the clearest and most responsible way to communicate what the data actually shows?”

Final exit question

Before leaving, answer this mentally—or ask several students to respond:

You have a dataset containing:

Product Category Product Price Customer Age Monthly Sales Advertising Spend

Which graph would you choose if your question was:

“Which category sells the most?”

Bar chart.

“What does the customer-age distribution look like?”

Histogram.

“Is advertising spending related to sales?”

Scatterplot.

“How have sales changed from January to December?”

Line chart.

“Are there unusually high product prices?”

A box plot could help identify potential outliers.

If you can choose the visualisation based on the analytical question—and explain why you chose it—then you have achieved the main learning outcome of today's lecture.
