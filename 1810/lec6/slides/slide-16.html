Box Plot

Approximately 7 minutes

The box plot is the way to highlight the median, quartiles, spread and potential outliers.

Imagine we arrange all exam scores from lowest to highest.

The median divides the data approximately in half.

Q1 marks approximately the 25th percentile.

Q3 marks approximately the 75th percentile.

The box therefore contains the middle 50% of the data.

That box gives us a very compact picture of where much of the dataset is concentrated.

Find the outliers

Imagine online transactions.

Most customers spend between £20 and £100.

One customer spends £5,000.

Is it an error?

Maybe.

Or perhaps it is a legitimate corporate customer.

So when analysts detect an outlier, the correct response is:

Investigate it.

Do not automatically delete it.

Comparing groups

Box plots become particularly powerful when placed side by side.

Imagine exam scores for:

Class A.

Class B.

Class C.

You can compare:

Median.

Spread.

Potential skew.

Potential outliers.

That is much more informative than simply comparing three averages.

Ask students

Suppose Class A and Class B both have a mean score of 70.

Does that mean their score distributions are identical?

No.

Class A might have scores tightly concentrated around 70.

Class B might contain many scores near 40 and many near 100.

The average alone hides that difference.

A box plot can reveal some of it.
