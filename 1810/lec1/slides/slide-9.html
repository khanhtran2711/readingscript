The slide gives the basic idea: create an analytics account and connect the website using a tag. We need to expand that into a process we can verify. A dashboard with no data can mean many things. Perhaps nobody used the site, but perhaps the tag points to the wrong destination. We should be able to distinguish those situations.

Before setup, look back at our business question. UniCart needs to understand progress through checkout. We have already identified events such as view_item, add_to_cart, begin_checkout, and purchase. A measurement plan states what we need the implementation to produce. That plan also gives us the expected result for testing.

Teaching cue: Setup steps are supported by [9]. Use the institution’s agreed settings for a real deployment. For the fictional Vietnam-based UniCart example, explain the choice of Vietnam reporting time and VND without changing a real property during this narration.

Why do time zone and currency deserve attention? Imagine one person reports orders by a Vietnamese calendar day while another uses a different time zone. Both may query the same underlying activity and produce different daily totals. Likewise, the number 600,000 needs a currency to make sense. Configuration choices affect the meaning of a report.

The account, property, and stream form an organisational structure. Think of an account as the organisation’s container, a property as the measurement environment for a business or product, and a stream as an incoming source such as its website. A property can receive more than one stream. We will keep our teaching example to one website so the path of the data is easy to follow. [25]

The installation method depends on the site. A CMS might provide an integration field. A developer can add the Google tag directly to the shared page template. Google Tag Manager is another option for managing tags and their rules. If Tag Manager handles our GA4 tagging, we should not independently add equivalent direct tracking calls without a clear design, because overlapping installations can create duplicate measurement. [10]

The original slide’s reference to placing a direct Google tag immediately after the opening head element remains relevant to manual installation. Many websites generate their pages from a common template, so the developer may make that change in one shared place. The tag then needs to load on the pages we intend to measure. [26]

Modern websites also change screens without a full reload. A single-page application may update the browser history while replacing the visible content. We need an agreed page-view strategy and a test that each meaningful screen change generates the intended record once. Automatic history-based measurement and manually sent page views must be coordinated. [19]

There is another correction to the slide. We should not expect a completely new person identifier on every page load. Where permitted and available, identifiers can persist so that records can be associated over time. However, the continuity has limits, as we discussed on the previous slides. Bounce rate is then calculated from sessions and engagement rules. The browser does not simply send “this visitor’s bounce rate” after each click.

Let’s inspect a deliberately simple implementation. I will open the test product page, add one H24 item to the basket, and inspect the resulting event. Before I click, tell me the name we expect and one parameter that would help us check it.

Teaching cue: Expected response is add_to_cart, with item_id H24, quantity 1, currency VND, or value 600000. Spend three minutes on the sequence below, including time for students to read the event details.

First, I will verify that I am viewing the correct teaching property. Realtime can help confirm that activity is arriving. For a closer look, I will use DebugView with debug mode enabled for this test device, for example through Tag Assistant or Tag Manager preview. I will perform one action and inspect the event and its parameters. A visit to the page alone does not enable DebugView automatically. [11]

Here is the result we expected: one successful basket update and one corresponding event. If I see two add_to_cart events after one update, I have a reason to investigate. Perhaps two listeners are attached, or two separate tagging implementations are sending the same action. The data may look active while still being wrong.

If I see no event, I will check whether the rule ran, whether the destination is correct, and whether the browser sent the request. Consent settings, privacy controls, and network errors can affect what appears. I will not silently weaken those controls to make the demonstration look successful.

For a real purchase, transaction_id helps identify the order. Our project should define one authoritative purchase trigger and a strategy for repeated deliveries or retries. We should also compare analytics purchases with backend orders using compatible dates and definitions. A purchase event describes an agreed business event; the finance system may have further rules for cancellations and refunds. [6]
