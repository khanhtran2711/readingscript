Slide 1

Data and Web Analytics

Teaching cue: 00 to 03. Display the title slide. Allow about 45 seconds for the opening show of hands and a few short answers. Write “UniCart” on the board.

Before we begin, think about the last time you looked at something online and decided not to buy it. Perhaps it was a pair of headphones, a concert ticket, or food delivery. Put your hand up if you have ever added something to a basket and then left without paying.

There are usually several hands for this question. Your basket may have a more ambitious budget than you do.

What stopped you? Perhaps the delivery charge appeared at the end. Perhaps the payment failed. Perhaps you were comparing prices and never intended to buy immediately. All those situations can produce the same business result: no completed order. However, they require different responses from the business.

Today, you are the analysts for UniCart, a fictional shop selling student essentials. The manager tells you, “People visit our website, but too few complete a purchase. Can you find out what is happening?”

Before we recommend a redesign or a discount, we need evidence. We need to know which actions the website recorded, how it recorded them, and whether those records are trustworthy.

By the end of today, you should be able to explain a visitor’s recorded journey, propose the events we need, and describe how to check a basic Google Analytics 4 implementation. You will also practise saying where the evidence ends and an assumption begins. That distinction matters whenever an analyst gives advice that someone else will act on.

Transition to slide 2: Let’s begin with the activity that produces our evidence. What happens, in data terms, when someone opens a website and starts using it?
