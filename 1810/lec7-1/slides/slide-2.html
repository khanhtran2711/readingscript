From Histogram to Normal Distribution

Approximately 6 minutes

On this slide, the frequency of continuous data is plotted against the values, and a bell-shaped probability distribution is shown over the data.

Student question

Suppose I shade half of a perfectly symmetrical normal distribution.

Approximately what probability would that represent?

50%.

Exactly.

Probability is therefore closely connected to area under the curve.

Lecturer Clarification

The slide states that histograms assume this bell shape.

Be careful with that interpretation.

Not every histogram is bell-shaped.

A histogram simply displays the distribution of data.

It might be:

symmetrical,

skewed,

bimodal,

approximately uniform,

or irregular.

Only when the underlying data are approximately normally distributed do we expect this familiar bell shape.

So I want you to remember:

Histogram = a tool for seeing the distribution.

Normal distribution = one possible shape of that distribution.
