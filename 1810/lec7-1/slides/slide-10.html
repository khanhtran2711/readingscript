Z-Score and the Standard Normal Distribution

Approximately 6 minutes

The PowerPoint introduces standardisation using the Z-score and notes that, in the standard normal distribution, the centre lies at zero.

The formula is:

[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} ]

Take your value.

Subtract the mean.

Then divide by the standard deviation.

The result tells you:

how many standard deviations the observation is away from the mean.

Z = 0

Exactly at the mean.

Z = +1

One standard deviation above the mean.

Z = +2

Two standard deviations above.

Z = −1

One standard deviation below.

Z = −2

Two standard deviations below.

The sign tells us direction.

The magnitude tells us distance.

Why standardise?

Consider two students.

Student A:

Exam score = 80.

Class mean = 70.

SD = 5.

Student B:

Exam score = 90.

Class mean = 80.

SD = 20.

At first glance:

90 > 80.

So perhaps Student B performed better.

But relative to their classes:

Student A:

[ z=\frac{80-70}{5}=2 ]

Student B:

[ z=\frac{90-80}{20}=0.5 ]

Student A performed 2 standard deviations above their class mean.

Student B performed only 0.5 standard deviations above their class mean.

So Z-scores allow us to compare values measured relative to different distributions.
