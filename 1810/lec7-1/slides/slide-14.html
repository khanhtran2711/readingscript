Slide 14

Central Limit Theorem

Approximately 7 minutes

The slide introduces the Central Limit Theorem, or CLT.

Its central idea is that, “The Central Limit Theorem does not say that the original data must be normally distributed. It says that if we repeatedly take sufficiently large random samples and calculate their means, the distribution of those sample means will tend to follow a normal distribution.”

That sentence is important, but it can sound very abstract.

So let's build it slowly.

Imagine a university with:

20,000 students.

You want to know:

the average amount students spend on food each week.

Could you interview all 20,000?

Possible perhaps, but expensive and inconvenient.

Instead, take a random sample of:

50 students.

Calculate their average weekly food spending.

Maybe:

£42.

Now take another random sample of 50.

Average:

£46.

Another sample:

£44.

Another:

£41.

Another:

£45.

Keep doing this.

Notice what we are collecting now.

Not individual spending values.

We are collecting:

sample means.

If we plot all of those sample means, that creates a:

sampling distribution of the mean.
