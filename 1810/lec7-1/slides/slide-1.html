Continuous Data Normal Distribution

Approximately 3 minutes

Good morning everyone.

In the previous lecture, we talked about data visualisation, and one of the graphs we studied was the histogram.

Remember what a histogram helps us understand?

It shows us the distribution of continuous numerical data.

But this lecture we are going one step further.

Imagine that we collect the heights of 10 students.

The histogram might look quite irregular.

Now imagine collecting the heights of 100 students.

Then 1,000 students.

Then perhaps 100,000 people.

We may begin to see a recognisable pattern.

In many situations, values tend to cluster around a central value, while fewer observations appear as we move further away from that centre.

This produces the famous:

bell-shaped curve.

Today we are going to investigate that shape and connect it to several important statistical concepts:

normal distribution,

standard deviation,

skewness,

kurtosis,

Z-scores,

probability,

percentiles,

and finally the Central Limit Theorem.

Don't worry if that sounds like a lot.

These ideas actually connect to one another.
